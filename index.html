Grow a Dwarf Galaxy

Set the flat speed v, the spin λ, the redshift at which growth ends and the profile exponent n. The model of From a primordial fluctuation to an idealised rotation curve returns the core at every epoch in four explicit steps and draws the idealised rotation curve today. Pick any of the 79 SPARC dwarfs of the paper and try to grow it; the list shows each galaxy’s best-fitting n (marked ~ where the fit does not constrain it) and where growth must end (zend) at median spin. With n = 2 this is the paper’s P18 profile with a flat core; n < 2 gives an inner cusp, ρ ∝ Rα with slope α = n − 2 (−1 is the NFW cusp), shown in the density panel.

ρ(R) = v²/(4πGR²) · [1−(1−x)e−x]n,  x = R/rc,  rc = Rb/y,  c ln y + A y = c(1−3λ²−ε) − CE

The galaxy’s fitted v and rc follow the chosen n. z_end in the list is for λ = 0.035, ε = 0 and the current n. “Solve” finds the redshift at which growth must end, for the current λ and ε, to give the galaxy’s fitted core. ~ before n: not constrained (error above 1 or at a bound of the fit); the paper uses the 49 constrained values.

Read zend with care: the assumed spin, a spin term not stored in the core, a profile with n = 1.76, or a core that keeps growing after growth ends move the median epoch within z ≈ 0.3–1.1, and the predicted spin dependence of the core is not seen in the data (paper, Sects. 6.4 and 6.5).

Flat ΛCDM with Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7, H0 = 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. Growth law Rb = v tacc/[(1+3π/2)(1+cΛℓ0)] with cΛ = 9.640. For exponent n the profile’s mass excess is c(n) = ∫(uⁿ−1)dx (¼ for n = 2, 0 for n = 1) and its energy constant CE(n) (−0.917 for n = 2); the core equation c ln y + A y = c(1−3λ²−ε) − CE, A = 3λ² + ε + ⅔ℓ, is solved with the Lambert W function. After growth rc ∝ (y + c)⁻¹. Galaxy fits: v and rc refitted for each n (errors as in the paper). The absolute core is uncertain by a factor of about two; the scalings with v and epoch are not.
